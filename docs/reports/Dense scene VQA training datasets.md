# Build VisualOverload's missing training set from parts

No public dataset copies VisualOverload's format: dense ~4K paintings, knowledge-free multiple-choice questions, paired yes/no scored pairwise, and freeform counts and OCR. The datasets that come closest in subject matter are art datasets with instance labels (DEArt, Materials In Paintings, PoPArt, IconArt), and these are also the most likely to leak test images. A small Qwen2.5-VL therefore has to be trained on a **mix assembled per skill**. The pieces are high-resolution photo and visual-search QA (TreeVGR-RL-37K, ZwZ-RL-VQA, FineRS-4k, VisualProbe), dense counting with point labels (PixMo-Points, FSC-147, crowd-crop tiles), compositional scene and activity labels converted into multiple choice and logically opposite yes/no pairs (GQA, VAW, SWiG/HICO-DET, Places365, VCR), dense scene-text and Latin/Dutch manuscript OCR (HierText, TextOCR, CATMuS), and a small painting-domain slice that you deduplicate or generate yourself from CC0 museum scans. For the three-pipeline comparison, the most useful finding is that open crop/zoom trajectories (Mini-o3, Chain-of-Focus, Pixel Reasoner, DeepEyes) and Python-interpreter trajectories (Thyme, PyVision-RL, CodeDance, CodeVision) already exist with verifiable answers. Nearly all of them are built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, and **no agentic model has published VisualOverload results**, so the base vs. ReAct vs. Python question is open. Contamination is manageable because the 150 test images are public on Hugging Face and listed by title in the paper's Table 4. That allows a combined metadata, perceptual-hash, embedding and tile-level filter, and it should run before any data reaches a GPU. On a 4×A6000 cluster with eight weeks left, the practical plan is this: SFT 3B (and optionally 7B with LoRA) on roughly 20–40k examples, train each agent on matched cold-start trajectories, run one short GRPO stage on 3B with rule-based rewards only, and measure everything on a proxy dev set that includes ~300 questions the team writes on non-test paintings. The rate-limited VisualOverload server is used only for final numbers.

**How to read the tags.** The notes behind this report tag every fact, and those tags are kept here. **[V]** means checked against a primary page (HF card or API, GitHub, arXiv). **[V-2nd]** means checked only on a secondary or aggregator page. **[P]** means the figure comes from the paper as remembered and was not re-fetched. **[U]** or **UNVERIFIED** means not confirmed. **Conflict** marks cases where the HF row counts disagree with the paper. Treat every [U] license as research-only until you have checked it.

## VisualOverload rewards perception in crowds, not art knowledge

The benchmark has **150 public-domain paintings from Google Arts & Culture, downsampled to the pixel count of 4K (3840×2160) with aspect ratio kept, and 2,720 hand-written questions**. Its 28 smaller images are all above Full HD ([arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25339v3)). The categories are heavily skewed: **scene 1,388 (51%), counting 559, reasoning 356, activity 150, attributes 149 and OCR 118** ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25339)). Scene yes/no questions come in logically opposite pairs ("Is it day?" / "Is it night?"), and **a pair scores only if both answers are correct** ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25339)). OCR answers are scored by exact match after aggressive normalization: diacritics, punctuation and spaces are stripped, text is lowercased, and Latin V→U and J→I ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25339)). Ground truth is private and scored through a rate-limited server, and the authors provide **no dev split**, expecting models to solve the tasks without fine-tuning ([arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25339v2)). The HF release (`paulgavrikov/visualoverload`) is CC BY-SA 4.0. Its single `test` split includes the images, which is what makes decontamination possible ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/paulgavrikov/visualoverload)) [V]. A second copy sits at `Voxel51/VisualOverload` ([HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Voxel51/VisualOverload)).

The starting points are low enough to leave room for a course project to show something. **Qwen2.5-VL-3B scores 54.1 overall and 4.8 on the hard split; 7B scores 61.5 and 9.6.** By category for 3B / 7B: activity 60.7 / 63.3, attributes 61.7 / 69.1, **reasoning 43.9 / 49.8** and scene 77.5 / 85.3 ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25339)) [V]. On counting, even the best model, Gemini 2.0 Flash, reaches only 41.7%. Models **undercount more and more as the true count rises**, and a 10% tolerance adds only 1.6 points ([arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25339v2)). OCR accuracy follows input resolution closely. Typical failures are reading-order errors and "autocorrection" toward likely words (e.g., "accidunt" → "accident"), and Qwen2.5-VL-72B leads at 72.9% ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25339)). The paper also reports that dedicated high-resolution models did *worse* than ordinary models of similar size, and that no tool-using or zoom model was evaluated ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25339)).

Several design choices follow from these numbers. The scene-pair score is the largest lever by item count, so training should teach **polarity consistency** and not only single yes/no accuracy. Counting data must cover the **10–80 range**, because TallyQA, CountBench and PixMo-Count eval sets stop at 10–15. OCR rewards should **copy VisualOverload's normalization exactly**, so the model is not rewarded for fluent but wrong text. Two quantities are unknown: the exact count distribution of the 559 counting questions, and whether "total" is a micro- or macro-average. Only 118 OCR questions exist, so each one is worth about 0.85 points of OCR accuracy, and any claimed gain needs bootstrap confidence intervals.

## Candidate datasets, grouped by the VisualOverload skill they train

Contamination risk below means **risk of sharing an artwork with VisualOverload's 150 test images**. VisualOverload's answers are private, so in most cases the danger is that the model has seen the image, not that it has seen the answer. The exception is instance-labelled art data, where boxes on a test painting turn directly into counting answers. All resolutions are as reported; nobody measured image sizes for this report.

The evidence on rewards is unusually clear. **CrowdVLM-R1 found that plain 0/1 exact-match GRPO did worse than SFT** (3B MAE 32.44 vs 29.89), while a graded relative-error reward beat both (25.16) ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.03724v2)). Perception-R1 got +17.9% on PixMo-Count using a Hungarian-matched point-F1 reward ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.07954)). "Getting to the Point" found that **outputting points before the count** beat direct counting and generalized better out of distribution ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21746)). Two counting sources should **not** be trusted: "CA-44" could not be found, and a claim that MGPO trained on FSC-147/PixMo-Count is UNVERIFIED.

For OCR RL, the notes recommend **reward = VisualOverload-normalized exact match + λ·(1 − NED) + a format term**, with exact match dominant. String-similarity rewards can be gamed by "hallucinated corrections" ([TextPecker](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.20903)), which is the same autocorrection failure VisualOverload penalizes. An OCR-focused GRPO study also found that full and LoRA SFT degraded multilingual understanding while reward-constrained GRPO improved it ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.18484)). Avoid LAION-derived OCR corpora such as MARIO-10M ([review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.14774)), since LAION contains many Google Arts & Culture reproductions.

The cleanest painting-domain option is **CC0 museum open access**: Rijksmuseum, the Met, NGA Washington, Art Institute of Chicago, Cleveland, SMK, Smithsonian, Yale Center for British Art and others ([georgeblck/art-datasets list](https://github.com/georgeblck/art-datasets)) [V-2nd]. The claim that Google Arts & Culture has no bulk API is UNVERIFIED. No published art-domain synthetic dense-scene VQA set exists, so this would be a small contribution of its own.

## Agent trajectories exist for both the ReAct and the Python pipeline

The three-way comparison is feasible mainly because tool-use training data is plentiful and mostly built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B. The interfaces fall into three families. **Fixed JSON crop tools** return a new image: DeepEyes, Mini-o3, CoF, Pixel Reasoner, OpenVisTool. These match the ReAct agent (P2). **Free-form Python in a sandbox** returns stdout and rendered images: Thyme, DeepEyesV2, PyVision-RL, CodeDance, CodeVision, CodeV. On the same evaluation harness (the PyVision-RL table, Qwen2.5-VL-7B base 78.5 V\*), the **best Python agent and the best crop agent are essentially tied on V\*: PyVision-Image 88.7 and Mini-o3 88.2**. DeepEyes reaches 85.6 and Thyme 82.2 ([PyVision-RL arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.20739)). Code agents gain more on math. Base-model V\* scores differ by about 7 points across papers (71.2–78.5), so only compare within one table. Thyme's **OCRBench fell from 88.4 to 86.3** after code-tool training ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.11630)), which matters for VisualOverload's OCR category. Pixel Reasoner reports 74% on TallyQA-Complex ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15966)), the only counting-specific agent result in the notes. Released 3B agent checkpoints are rare: GRIT-3B and VTool-R1-3B were found, and none for DeepEyes, Mini-o3, Thyme or PyVision (the search was not exhaustive).

The literature also documents three known ways agent training goes wrong, and the comparison should measure all three. **Interaction collapse**: RL on code agents tends to reduce tool use, which PyVision-RL counters with an accumulative tool reward of 0.1 per turn ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20739)). **Over-use**: AdaTooler-V finds open models call tools when they don't need to ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16918)). **Unfaithful tool use**: CodeV finds high accuracy alongside unfaithful crops ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19661)), and an audit across six models found that returned crops often have **no causal effect on the answer** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06270)). Cold-start evidence is mixed. DeepEyes reports tool use emerging from RL alone, while DeepEyesV2 says direct RL "fails to induce robust tool-use behavior" for code and search without a cold start ([DeepEyesV2 arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05271)). Because P3 needs a cold start, give P2 one as well, so both agents start from the same footing.

## A prioritised training mix sized for four A6000s and eight weeks

Compute sets the plan. The DeepEyes authors recommend **≥32 GPUs and ≥1,200 GB CPU RAM per node** for 7B agentic RL with a vLLM-served Qwen2.5-72B judge ([GitHub](https://github.com/Visual-Agent/DeepEyes)). VTool-R1 trained 3B on 8×H100 and 7B on 16×H100, mostly converging within **50–100 steps** ([GitHub](https://github.com/VTool-R1/VTool-R1)). PyVision-RL used 700 RL steps on 8×H100 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.20739)). A 4×A6000 cluster cannot host a 72B judge next to policy rollouts, so the plan below uses **only rule-checkable rewards**: MC letter, integer, normalized string. It runs **RL only on 3B**, with 7B limited to LoRA SFT if time allows (own estimate, not from the notes). Qwen2.5-VL's dynamic resolution can take ~4K input once `max_pixels` is raised ([high-res notes, after GeoLLaVA](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21375v2)). By my estimate a full-resolution 8.3 MP image costs ~10k visual tokens (28-px merged patches) [own estimate]. Cap the global view at roughly 1–2 MP during RL and let the agents' crops supply the detail. Crops are the point of P2 and P3 in the first place.

The key design rule for a fair comparison is to **build one shared question pool and render it three ways**. P1 sees plain QA (trajectories stripped, which the high-res notes explicitly endorse). P2 sees crop-tool trajectories. P3 sees Python trajectories. All three run GRPO on the same prompts with the same rewards. Differences in results can then be attributed to the pipeline rather than the data.

## Proxy evaluation: assemble a dev set before touching the server

The VisualOverload server is rate-limited and offers no dev split, so all model selection has to happen on proxies. No public proxy contains dense paintings at 4K, so **the most valuable single investment is a team-written painting dev set**. Pick 15–20 CC0 high-resolution crowded genre or market scenes by artists absent from Table 4 (e.g., Teniers, Ostade, Vinckboons, Valckenborch, van de Venne). ## A decontamination procedure keyed to Table 4

VisualOverload's images are public and its titles are listed, so the team can filter at four levels that catch progressively subtler matches. The notes' thresholds are starting points to tune on known positives, not tested values. FineVision's precedent used SSCD embeddings at cosine ≥0.95 against 66 benchmarks and found 1.02% contamination ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17269)).

| Step | What to do | What it catches |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Reference set | Download all 150 images from `paulgavrikov/visualoverload` ([HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/paulgavrikov/visualoverload)). **Re-parse Table 4 programmatically** from the arXiv HTML/LaTeX into (artist, title, date, GA&C link) | Ground truth for every later step |
| 1. Subset drops | Remove whole subsets: Vision-Flan/`vflan` tasks named `WIKIART*`, `semart*`, `PACS+art_painting*`, `DOMAIN_NET+painting*`; `sharegpt4v(knowledge)`; LLaVA-OV-1.5 `wit` and `wikipedia_2m`; all WikiArt/WGA-based art sets you are not deliberately using | Known art-bearing mixtures ([LLaVA-OV parquet](https://huggingface.co/datasets/lmms-lab/LLaVA-OneVision-Data/tree/main)) |
| 2. Metadata + text filter | Fuzzy-match (artist, title, date) against Table 4. Block **all** Bruegel Elder/Younger versions of listed compositions and **all impressions** of the listed Bruegel engravings. For a conservative painting slice, drop Bruegel, Avercamp, Aertsen/Beuckelaer and Steen entirely. Grep captions and QA text for test titles ("Hunters in the Snow", "Netherlandish Proverbs", "Night Watch", "Grande Jatte") | Copies, other impressions, text referencing test works |
| 3. Global near-duplicates | pHash/dHash at several resizes, plus SSCD and CLIP/SigLIP or DINOv2 embeddings. Flag cosine ≥~0.90 for manual review and auto-drop ≥0.95 | Re-scans, recompressions, colour shifts |
| 4. Crop- and composition-level | Tile each test image (3×3 and 5×5) and search training images against the tiles. Also run local-feature matching (SIFT/ORB or SuperPoint+LightGlue with RANSAC inlier counts) on the remaining art-domain candidates | **Detail crops** (WGA, museum sites, and crops in visual-search sets), partial views, print impressions with different paper tone |
| 5. Dev-set hygiene | Run the same filter between training images and dev images (V\*, HR-Bench, HRScene, your painting dev set) | Inflated proxy scores |
| 6. Report | Log the number of items removed per dataset and per step, and the thresholds used | Reviewer and grader questions |

Order the work by risk. Run steps 2–4 in full on any **instance-labelled art set** (DEArt, MIP, PoPArt, People-Art, Human-Art, IconArt, ArtDL), because a surviving test painting there leaks counting labels. Next come web-crawled pools (ZwZ's LAION/MetaCLIP/CC12M images, PixMo, VisualProbe, Thyme-RL, DeepEyesV2, CoTA and the LLaVA-NeXT-derived VGR/TreeVGR-SFT) and any CC0 museum dump used for synthetic QA. Photo-only sources (COCO, VG, SA-1B, VisDrone, Open Images, street view, manuscripts) need only the hash pass. Nobody has yet run an overlap check on any of these datasets, and no published contamination analysis of VisualOverload exists. Treat every risk rating above as a prediction for step 3–4 to confirm or refute.

## Conclusion

The data landscape points to a sharper framing of the project. VisualOverload is a test of **transfer from photos to paintings**: the skills it needs (dense counting, small-text reading, compositional referring, logical consistency) all have mature photo datasets, but none in the painting domain, and the painting datasets that do exist mostly train the wrong skill (art-historical recall) or leak the test images. A course project that holds the question pool fixed and varies only the inference pipeline (plain, crop-tool, Python) can say something no published work has yet said, because no agentic model has been scored on VisualOverload. Its most likely surprise is in the scene-pair and OCR categories. There, tool use offers little extra detail, code-tool training has already been shown to cost OCR accuracy, and a pair-consistency reward may matter more than any tool.

Three choices will decide how much the final numbers can be trusted. The decontamination log must exist before training starts. The painting dev set must be written by the team rather than borrowed. And the crop-black control must accompany every agent result, so that a gain on VisualOverload can be attributed to looking more closely and not to more tokens or to having seen famous paintings before. The open risks are unverified licenses ([U] throughout), conflicts between HF row counts and the papers, and the untested assumption that COCO-style photo training transfers to 16th-century Flemish crowds. The team-written painting dev set is the cheapest way to test that last assumption early.
