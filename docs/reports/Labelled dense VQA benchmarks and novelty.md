# Replace VisualOverload with evidence-boxed dense benchmarks

No single public benchmark reproduces VisualOverload: painted crowds at about 4K, six categories and three difficulty levels. Five benchmarks with public per-question answers cover it between them, and two of them do something VisualOverload never could. The headline suite should be **TreeBench (405 questions, 10 categories, a ground-truth box for every target), ZoomBench (845, a box and evidence crop for every item), VisualProbe Easy/Medium/Hard (515, a built-in difficulty split on 4K images), a dense-scene slice of MME-RealWorld-Lite (about 800: crowd and vehicle monitoring, driving, OCR in the wild) and a 500-question stratified sample of CountQA**. That is about 3,100 test questions, all labelled. Every one of them leaves real headroom for an 8B model: Qwen3-VL-8B scores 48.3 on TreeBench, 37.9 on ZoomBench and 31.6 on VisualProbe-Hard. V\*, by contrast, is saturated at 86–93. The evidence boxes are what make the project novel. Published work has compared fixed-tool and code agents only with OpenCV operations (VTC-Bench), on 100 counting images with one closed model (VTOS), or with text-only planners (m&m's). Faithfulness audits of tool-using VLMs cover crop/zoom agents only. So the primary contribution should be **an evidence-grounded interface comparison**: base VLM vs ReAct vs Python agent, with the same open model, the same specialist tool backends and matched budgets. Every crop, detector query and OCR region is scored against the ground-truth box, and the tool outputs are perturbed to test whether answers depend on them. Two cheap secondary angles hang off the same runs: a target-size and density dose-response curve, and a leave-one-tool-out ablation with oracle and noisy tools. Run **Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct as the workhorse** (bf16, one GPU, the most published reference numbers). Run the **cached Qwen3.8-27B-FP8 as the scale and de-risking arm** on the other GPU, after a week-1 smoke test confirms it takes images and runs FP8 on Ampere. Hold Gemma-4-31B in reserve: in bf16 it occupies both GPUs and leaves no room for the tools. Drop RL, and keep SFT as a stretch goal only.

**How to read the tags.** The tags from the underlying notes are kept. **[V]** means checked this session against a primary page: the Hugging Face API or card, a downloaded and counted annotation file, or the arXiv HTML. **[N]** means carried over from earlier project notes without re-fetching. **[U]** or **UNVERIFIED** means not confirmed. **(abstract)** and **(snippet only)** mark claims read only from an arXiv abstract or a search snippet. Anything labelled "own estimate" is mine and was not measured. Background on training data and decontamination is in the earlier report, `docs/reports/Dense scene VQA training datasets.md`, and is not repeated here.

## Five labelled benchmarks cover what VisualOverload measured

VisualOverload's problem for this project is structural. Its 2,720 answers are private, there is no dev split, and the scoring server is rate-limited per user per day. Per-question correctness, per-category breakdowns, failure cases and tool-use-versus-correctness tables are therefore all out of reach. The replacements below all publish answers for every item. They were ranked on category labels, density, a difficulty axis, evidence boxes, headroom for an 8B model, cost and license.

Qwen3-VL-8B scores come from the InSight-o3 table ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.18745)) and, for ZoomBench, from ZwZ Table 2 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11858)).

**TreeBench is the anchor.** It is the only set that combines labelled categories, an evidence box for every question, dense scenes and a cheap size of 405. Its categories are uneven, from **OCR 68 and Perspective Transform 85 down to Material 13 and Object Retrieval 16** [V]. Perspective Transform is the hardest category for every model, 11.8–22.4%, and o3 sits at the top of that range ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07999)) [V]. That category is a ready-made test of whether tools help reasoning at all or only perception. **ZoomBench is the best set for asking "did the agent look in the right place?"** It ships a box and an evidence crop for every item, and its base scores are very low: Qwen3-VL-8B 37.87, Gemini-3-Flash 59.29 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11858)) [V]. Its six paper dimensions (counting, OCR, colour, structure, material, object identification) are **not** in the released fields [V], so the team has to tag items, either with an LLM classifier plus a spot-check or by falling back to the `question_type` field (MC vs numeric). **VisualProbe gives the project the explicit Easy/Medium/Hard split** that VisualOverload had. It has no category field and needs manual tagging. Its answers are short free-form strings, so they need normalized matching plus a judge for near-misses.

**MME-RealWorld-Lite is the closest match to VisualOverload's activity and crowd-counting questions.** The full English file has 23,609 items [V, counted]. Its Monitoring domain has **964 person-counting and 353 vehicle-counting items, plus person orientation and intention**, and its Autonomous Driving domain has pedestrian motion and intention subtasks ([HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/yifanzhang114/MME-RealWorld)) [V]. Lite keeps "50 samples per task, or all if fewer than 50" and is supported by VLMEvalKit and lmms-eval ([GitHub](https://github.com/yfzhang114/MME-RealWorld)) [V]. Its exact size, about 2,000 if a task is a Subtask×Category group, has not been checked against the zip. **CountQA supplies the dense counting that the other sets lack.** It has real cluttered photos with ground-truth counts from 0 to 400 (mean 12.1), and the best of 15 models (Gemini 2.5 Pro) reaches only 42.93% exact match ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06585)) [V]. The sets map onto VisualOverload's six categories unevenly. Attributes, counting, OCR and spatial reasoning are well covered. **Activity is weakly covered**: only MME-RW pedestrian and monitoring intention, and COREVQA's action-heavy statements, come close. Scene-level questions survive only as UR-Bench's Global level and remote-sensing land-use items. Treat activity as a known hole, not something to paper over.

One benchmark deserves a special flag. **VisualNeedle** has 300 questions on very dense images: a mean of 7.46 MP, 122.79 detected entities per image, and 297 of 300 targets covering ≤1% of the image. It also reports tools-versus-no-tools per category ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26380)) [V]. It would be the best single benchmark for this project, but its data is "provided in the supplementary material" with no HF or GitHub release found [V], and its license is unclear. Spend an hour in week 1 trying the arXiv supplementary files and emailing the authors. If it arrives, it goes straight into the core suite. If not, do not plan around it.

## Saturation, licenses and contamination decide what can be a headline

**V\* is finished as a headline metric.** Qwen3-VL-8B scores 85.9–86.4, released agents score 82–89, and ZwZ-4B reaches 92.67 ([ZwZ](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11858), [CounterCredit](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22910)) [V]. HR-Bench and MME-RealWorld are mid-range. Their problem is noise rather than saturation: **the same Qwen3-VL-8B is reported at 65.96 on MME-RW-en in one paper and 60.7 in another** ([ZwZ](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11858), [CounterCredit](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22910)) [V]. Base-versus-agent gaps of a few points sit inside that cross-paper spread, so the team must rerun every baseline in its own harness and never compare its agents with numbers copied from papers. The skill-specific sets have the same ceiling problem. CountBench sits at 80–95 for Qwen2.5/3-VL ([Qwen3-VL report](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.21631)) and TextVQA at about 85 ([Qwen2.5-VL report](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.13923)). COREVQA's always-"false" baseline of **72.1% beats Qwen2.5-VL-72B's 69.95** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.13405v1)) [V], so it can only be reported with balanced accuracy or F1.

**Licenses are fine for a course project but untidy.** The HF tags for TreeBench, ZoomBench, MME-RealWorld (full), UR-Bench and RSHR-Bench are Apache-2.0, and CountQA and HoloCount are CC BY 4.0 [V]. VisualProbe, V\*, MME-RealWorld-Lite, COREVQA and O3-Bench carry **no license tag**. HR-Bench is tagged "other" with a 0-byte LICENSE file, and FineRS-4k is just "cc" [V/N]. HRScene is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 and LRS-VQA is CC BY-NC 4.0 ([HF HRScene](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Wenliang04/HRScene), [HF LRS-VQA](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ll-13/LRS-VQA)) [V/N]. UR-Bench includes Google Street View panoramas, whose terms may restrict redistribution despite the repo's Apache tag (inference). The rule is simple: evaluate locally, never re-host images, and list every dataset's terms in an appendix of the final report.

**Contamination matters only if the team evaluates released agent checkpoints or fine-tunes.** In those cases it matters a great deal. MGPO post-trained on 21K MME-RealWorld samples ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.05920)) [N]. DeepEyesV2's RL repo ships a 191-row `vstar_test.parquet`, the exact size of V\* ([HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/honglyhly/DeepEyesV2_RL)) [V]; whether it was trained on or only used for validation is **[U]**. Mini-o3 trained on VisualProbe-train ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.07969)) [N], and TreeVGR was co-designed with TreeBench. ZwZ's RL pool includes SA-1B, the image source of both V\* and TreeBench ([HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/inclusionAI/ZwZ-RL-VQA)) [N].

If the project builds its own agents on an untouched base model, contamination reduces to the base model's pretraining. That cannot be ruled out because Qwen3-VL's corpus is undisclosed. One base-model fact does matter, though: per the MED paper, **Qwen3-VL-8B was trained with crop-and-zoom** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01334v2)) [V]. Its P2 crop gains are therefore partly native skill, not just harness design.

The training-set picture interacts with these benchmarks in one specific way. The earlier report's training mix includes VisualProbe-train, FineRS-4k train and TreeVGR data. If the stretch SFT uses them, VisualProbe and FineRS become **in-distribution** and must be reported separately. The perceptual-hash and embedding filter described there must also be re-pointed from VisualOverload's Table 4 to the new evaluation images, above all the SA-1B images behind TreeBench.

Public labels also create a new risk: **tuning prompts on the test set**. VisualOverload's private ground truth enforced discipline by accident, and the replacement suite does not. Develop and tune every prompt, tool docstring and turn budget on **V\* (191) plus the `cycle_category == 0` slice of HR-Bench 4K (about 200 questions)**. V\* is saturated anyway, so it is worthless as a headline but perfect as a dev set. Then freeze the configuration and run the core suite once. If a benchmark-specific bug forces a look at core data, carve out a fixed 10% dev slice and exclude it from every reported number.

## Nobody has compared ReAct and Python agents on identical specialist tools

The project's core comparison is the same open VLM, the same crop, detection, segmentation, pose and OCR backends, and a ReAct interface against a Python interface on dense VQA. As of 15 October 2026 this comparison **appears unpublished**, and the three nearest studies each miss a different piece. **VTC-Bench** compares interface-based with code-based invocation on the same models, but over **32 OpenCV operations** on 680 problems, not specialist models or dense photos. It finds the two "comparable overall", and most Qwen3-VL variants **decline or barely change** with tools; Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking loses 3.97 points with code ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15030v1)). **VTOS** (EMNLP 2026) runs ReAct against CodeAct with Grounding-DINO, but on **100 LVIS counting images with one closed model**. It finds CodeAct better than ReAct (MAE 39.38 vs 46.31) and **both worse than Claude Sonnet 4.6 with no tools (35.13)** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.20728)). **m&m's** compares JSON and code plans, but with text-only LLM planners ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.11085v4)).

The big "thinking with images" families (DeepEyes, Mini-o3 and Chain-of-Focus for crop; Thyme, PyVision, CodeV and CodeVision for code) change the data, the reward and the base model together, so they cannot isolate the interface. CodeV concedes that its comparison is not controlled ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.19661)). Prior evidence also points in opposite directions. Text CodeAct ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.01030)) and VTOS favour code, while m&m's and VTC-Bench find parity or a JSON advantage. **Either outcome is therefore publishable-quality information.** That matters for a course project, because a null result still counts.

The 2026 faithfulness audits sharpen the gap further. The Illusion audit finds that **70.7% of Qwen3-VL-8B's V\* tool episodes are "Calling Without Looking"**: the observations have no causal effect on the answer. Even so, its tool gains are +6.9 on V\* and +11.7 on VisualProbe, against **−3.7 on MME-RW-Lite**. The audit's own stated limitation is that "only crop-and-zoom was studied" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06270)) [V]. CounterCredit finds that only **10–12% of visual calls were both needed and used** at a cold-start checkpoint, and that released agents make spurious calls 36–87% of the time ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22910)) [V]. CodeV measures faithful cropping with a **GPT-4o judge, not ground-truth boxes**. It reports Thyme, the only code agent ever audited, at 1–7% faithful and calling tools in under 10% of rollouts ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.19661)). VisualNeedle scores crop coverage against ground-truth boxes, but only for crop tools on mostly closed models ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26380)). ToolGate is the one study with a ReAct agent using OCR, detection and segmentation on Qwen3-VL. It finds **helpful and harmful calls at nearly equal rates, 11.8% vs 9.9%** (abstract) ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03054)). Every one of these audits corrupts *images*. None perturbs structured tool outputs such as detector boxes, OCR strings or keypoints, and none scores a code agent's pixel accesses against ground-truth evidence.

The per-category evidence on *when* tools help is fragmentary but consistent. VisualNeedle's crop tools gain most on **OCR (+26.55) and spatial (+21.58) and least on colour (+11.97)**, averaged over 9 models ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26380)) [V]. VTC-Bench's Tier 2 (measurement, colour, counting) is "mixed, with noticeable drops in high-precision tasks" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15030v1)). "Beyond Zooming" finds zoom plateaus on hard, dispersed-evidence tasks (abstract) ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25993)). Yet **no published analysis plots tool benefit against target-box area or object count across interfaces**. VisualNeedle reports area statistics but not gain by area, and MED has no size analysis at all [notes]. Specialist tools now mostly add localisation, not better answers. In VTOS, Grounding-DINO has far better mIoU than Qwen3-VL-8B (32.19 vs 3.38), but the two err in opposite directions: the detector overcounts (+27.44 bias) and the VLM undercounts (−54.26) ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.20728)). "Seeing Is Not Measuring" shows the detector is the bottleneck: object-size accuracy is 0.61 with a real detector and **0.97 with ground-truth boxes** (abstract) ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29073)). The notes found **no 2025–2026 paper evaluating a pose-estimation tool, or a PaddleOCR-style OCR engine, as a tool for Qwen3-VL, Qwen3.5 or Gemma 4 on dense VQA**.

## A box-scored interface study, plus two cheap add-ons

**The primary angle is an evidence-grounded comparison of the three pipelines.** P1 (base), P2 (ReAct with fixed tools) and P3 (Python agent) run on the same open model, with identical tool backends, identical tool docstrings, and matched turn and token budgets. The fixed tools are crop/zoom, Grounding-DINO or OWLv2, SAM2, a pose model and PaddleOCR. P3 receives the same functions as a Python API, plus NumPy and OpenCV. Every pipeline reads the image through one wrapped image object that logs every pixel region touched. That covers crops, detector query regions, OCR regions, and any array slicing done by P3 code. Each logged region is then scored by IoU and by coverage of the ground-truth evidence box on TreeBench and ZoomBench, and on FineRS where it is used.

Four interventions are applied on the boxed subset: **crop-black**, where returned crops are blacked out (VisualNeedle's control); **random patch**, where a random same-size patch replaces the real crop (CounterCredit's control); and two new ones, **shuffled detector boxes** and **perturbed OCR strings**. Each intervention measures whether answers causally depend on what came back. The outputs are four numbers per category: accuracy, evidence-hit rate, causal reliance and cost (calls, tokens, wall-clock). From them, the failure analysis the proposal promised can be auto-labelled from ground truth rather than eyeballed:

| Failure label | Rule |
|---|---|
| Wrong region | Best IoU below a threshold |
| Tool error | The tool's output disagrees with the ground truth |
| Misread output | Correct tool output, wrong answer |
| Over-calling | Calls after the evidence box was already covered |
| Under-calling | No call and a wrong answer that an oracle crop fixes |
| Calling without looking | The answer is invariant to corruption |

Validate the labels on about 200 hand-checked trajectories. The hypothesis to test comes from the notes. Code beats ReAct only where composition or calibration is needed: counting with threshold, NMS or tiling, multi-object comparison, measurement. ReAct ties or wins on single-region lookups (OCR, attributes). At 8B, P3's edge shrinks or reverses through format and execution errors, consistent with VTC-Bench's Qwen3-VL declines. The prediction on faithfulness is that P3 shows *lower* evidence-hit rates than P2 even when accuracy matches, because code tends to process the whole image. The closest prior work, and the reason each misses this, is the set named above: VTC-Bench (OpenCV, not dense), VTOS (counting, one closed model), VisualNeedle Appendix J (crop vs OpenCV, closed models, no code), and the Illusion audit, CodeV and CounterCredit (crop-only faithfulness).

**Secondary angle 1 is a target-size and density dose-response.** It replaces VisualOverload's difficulty axis with a better-defined one. Bin every boxed item by ground-truth box-area fraction (TreeBench, ZoomBench, FineRS's XXS <0.017% / XS / S tiers [V]), every counting item by ground-truth count (CountQA 0–400), and VisualProbe by its levels. Re-run P1 and P2 on TreeBench and ZoomBench with the input downsampled to 1K, 2K and native resolution. The deliverable is a curve of tool benefit against target size, one per interface. The hypothesis is that the benefit rises as targets shrink and counts grow, then saturates or reverses at extreme density, where VTOS shows detectors overcounting and VLMs undercounting. The crossover should differ between P2 and P3. The closest prior work covers only parts of this. Chain-of-Focus swept 8 resolutions on its own trained model, crop only ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15436)). VTOS used two density tiers for counting. VisualOverload found its InternVL3 gains plateau above Full HD with no tools ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25339)). None compares interfaces. The angle costs little: the notes estimate 20–40 GPU-hours, mostly reusing primary runs.

**Secondary angle 2 is leave-one-tool-out with oracle and noisy tools.** It asks which specialist tools still earn their place next to a native-grounding VLM. Run P2 with each tool removed in turn. Then swap the detector for an **oracle** that returns the ground-truth target boxes, the analogue of CrossHOI's "person box given" setting, where Qwen2.5-VL-7B's Macro-F1 rises from 29.73 to 48.93 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.18753v4)). Finally, use a **noisy** detector whose boxes are jittered or dropped at controlled rates. This separates "the tool is useless" from "the tool is useful but our detector is bad", and it measures how much tool error the agent tolerates. The hypothesis is that only crop (OCR and small objects) and detection for counting carry positive marginal value, while segmentation, pose and the OCR engine add little over crop plus native OCR. The closest prior work is ToolGate (a learned gate, no per-tool ablation), VTOS (DINO/SAM only), MG-VQA (perception tools add only 36.2→37.6 on cluttered tabletops (abstract), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17129)) and "Seeing Is Not Measuring" (3D tools). Be honest about the pose tool. No public dense-scene VQA with keypoints exists, so its arm runs only on proxies: MME-RW pedestrian motion and intention, HoloCount's Action & State subset, and a balanced COREVQA sample. A finding that it never helps is likely and acceptable.

**Deliberately left out:** a thinking × tools factorial is a clean gap; no controlled study exists. But it doubles every run and needs token-budget sweeps to avoid truncation artifacts, so it is the first thing to add only if the team is ahead by mid-November. SFT is a stretch goal. RL should be dropped. DeepEyes recommends ≥32 GPUs for 7B agentic RL ([GitHub](https://github.com/Visual-Agent/DeepEyes)), and the notes rate even LoRA GRPO on 2× A6000 as high risk in seven weeks. If SFT happens, the one novel version is **interface-matched SFT**: the same teacher (the 27B model), the same questions and the same tools, with trajectories rendered once as P2 JSON and once as P3 code. That lets the team ask which interface a small student learns faster. Train with LoRA on Qwen3-VL-4B or 8B over a few thousand causally filtered trajectories, using data from the earlier report with the decontamination caveat above. Cost and Pareto numbers are logged anyway inside the primary angle and reported as a plot, not claimed as a separate contribution.

## Qwen3-VL-8B runs the study, the cached 27B checks scale

Model choice depends on three things: whether the agents can run at all on 2× RTX A6000, whether the harness can be checked against published numbers, and whether tools and model can share the hardware.

**Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct should be the workhorse.** In bf16 it is about 17 GB, so it fits on one A6000 with vLLM and leaves the second GPU free for Grounding-DINO/OWLv2, SAM2, the pose model and PaddleOCR (notes' estimate). It has more published reference points on exactly this suite than any other open model: TreeBench 48.3, VisualProbe-Hard 31.6 and MME-RW-Lite 53.0 ([InSight-o3](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.18745)), ZoomBench 37.87 ([ZwZ](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11858)), per-benchmark crop-tool deltas and Calling Without Looking rates ([Illusion audit](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06270)), and a dense-counting MAE of 54.26 ([VTOS](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.20728)). Week-1 P1 runs can therefore validate the harness: landing within about 3–5 points of these numbers is the bar, given the cross-paper noise above. The P2 crop-only configuration can also reproduce the Illusion audit before adding specialist tools. Its main weakness is zero-shot code. VTC-Bench shows Qwen3-VL scores mostly decline with code tools (26.32–34.41 vs base 29.56–36.32) ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15030v1)), so P3 at 8B may lose on formatting rather than reasoning. That is exactly why a second, larger arm is needed.

**The cached Qwen3.8-27B-FP8 is the right second arm. It replaces the frontier-API model the notes proposed for de-risking P3.** The notes contain **no published numbers for any "Qwen3.8" model**, and nothing in them confirms it has a vision encoder or a tool-calling chat template. That must be the first thing checked on 16 October. The nearest evidence for the family is HoloCount, where **Qwen3.5-27B averages 75.9 vs Qwen3-VL-8B's 58.1, and scores 20.0 vs 0.0 on High-Density counting** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.06420v1)) [V]. A 27B in FP8 is roughly 27–28 GB of weights, which fits one 48 GB A6000 with about 15–18 GB left for the KV cache (own estimate).

Two own-estimate cautions apply. The A6000 has no native FP8; my understanding is that vLLM serves FP8 checkpoints on Ampere through weight-only (W8A16) Marlin kernels that compute in 16-bit, and this must be confirmed on vLLM 0.26 with this exact checkpoint before committing **[U]**. Decode speed is the second: single-stream decode on a 768 GB/s A6000 is bandwidth-bound at very roughly 25–30 tokens/s for 27 GB of weights, against about 45 tokens/s for the 8B, so concurrency through vLLM batching across episodes is mandatory and long "thinking" traces should be off by default. With the 8B and the tools sharing GPU 1 (about 17 GB plus a few GB of tool models) and the 27B on GPU 0, **both model arms can run in parallel** (own estimate).

**Gemma-4-31B should stay in reserve.** In bf16 it is about 62 GB, so it needs tensor parallelism across both A6000s and evicts the tool backends entirely. Google advertises native function calling ([DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/models/gemma/gemma-4/)), but the notes found no vision-tool evaluation of Gemma 4. The only practitioner reports describe it sometimes answering from internal knowledge instead of calling a tool, and tool-calling glitches on the 26B variant (anecdotal, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47961107)). The single research mention, MG-VQA v1's Gemma-4 rising from 46.4 to 62.4 with tools, comes from a search summary and is absent from the v3 abstract **[U]**. Use Gemma-4-31B only if Qwen3.8-27B fails the smoke test, loaded with on-the-fly FP8 weight-only quantization to fit one GPU **[U, test first]**. **Qwen3.5-9B is the fallback small model** if Qwen3-VL-8B misbehaves in the harness. Two controls carry over from the VisualOverload notes and stay mandatory. First, fix and report the base model's `max_pixels`, and add a **tile-and-answer P1 control** (no agent, one pass over tiles) so that "more pixels" is separated from "agent reasoning". Otherwise a single 4K image costs on the order of 10K visual tokens and swamps the 27B's KV cache (own estimate, from the earlier report).

**Reworded research question and success criteria.** Replace the proposal's Section 1 question with this:

> On dense, high-resolution VQA benchmarks with public per-question labels, when — by skill category and by target size and scene density — does giving the same open VLM visual tools improve accuracy? Does a Python-interpreter interface beat a fixed-tool ReAct interface when both use identical tool backends, prompts and budgets? And do the agents' answers actually depend on evidence retrieved from the correct image region?

Replace Section 4's metric and success criterion as follows. The metric becomes "accuracy under each benchmark's official scoring, reported overall, per category and per target-size or difficulty bin, with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals and paired McNemar tests between pipelines; plus evidence-hit rate against ground-truth boxes, change under tool-output interventions, and calls, tokens and latency per question." The success criterion becomes:

> The study succeeds if (i) P1, P2 and P3 are evaluated reproducibly on about 3,000 labelled test questions, with P1 reproducing published Qwen3-VL-8B scores within about 5 points; (ii) per-category and per-size effects are reported with confidence intervals, and each difference is identified as significant or not; (iii) every agent result comes with its evidence-hit rate and its intervention sensitivity; and (iv) the failure taxonomy is validated on at least 200 hand-checked trajectories. A well-controlled finding that tools or code do not help counts as success.

Also amend Section 3: the datasets are now the Tier A sets above, and compute is 2× RTX A6000. Amend Section 4 to state that per-category differences under roughly 8–10 points are not resolvable with 13–85 items per TreeBench category or 105–270 per VisualProbe level, so categories will be pooled where needed.

## Conclusion

Losing VisualOverload's private labels turns out to strengthen the project. VisualOverload could only have told the team *whether* tools helped. TreeBench's and ZoomBench's evidence boxes let it measure *where* each agent looked and *whether the answer depended on what it saw*. That question is the active research front in 2026, and so far it has been studied only for crop-only agents, through image corruption. Extending it to code agents and to structured specialist-tool outputs is the contribution that no published paper has made. A small team can also defend it with a null result.

The main risks are concrete and front-loaded. First, the 27B model may turn out not to be a working vision-and-tool model on Ampere FP8; that is decided by 20 October. Second, the 8B model may fail P3 on formatting alone; the 27B arm and a fixed few-shot harness hedge that. Third, every label is public, so the team can drift into tuning on the test set; the V\*/HR-Bench dev tier and a 3 December freeze guard against that. Activity and painting-domain coverage remain thin and should be stated as limitations rather than stretched to fit. Re-check arXiv in mid-November, since several 2026 audits are one revision away from covering code agents.
